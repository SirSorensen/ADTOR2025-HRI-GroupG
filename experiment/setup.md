
# Research Question
Explore how audio and camera sensors can be combined for:
- Improve the robot’s ability to initiate and manage conversations.
- Implement faster, more human-like turn-taking.

Evaluate your improvements through a small user study.


# Constructs
 - ability to initiate conversations
 - ability to manage conversations
 - fast turn-taking
 - human-like turn-taking


# Hypotheses

- H1 (Initiative): Our improvements of *the robot's controller's code* to use the audio and camera sensors improves the robot's ability to initiate conversations from a user's perspective, compared to the robot's original version.
- H2 (Management): Our improvements of *the robot's controller's code* to use the audio and camera sensors improves the robot's ability to manage conversations from a user's perspective, compared to the robot's original version.
- H3 (Faster): Our improvements of *the robot's controller's code* to use the audio and camera sensors makes the robot's turn-taking faster from a user's perspective, compared to the robot's original version.
- H4 (Human-like): Our improvements of *the robot's controller's code* to use the audio and camera sensors makes the robot's turn-taking more human-like from a user's perspective, compared to the robot's original version.


# Design

Location: Testing in the field.

Materials & Methods:
 - 1 Experimenter
 - 1 Computer, able to run both robot versions. (Needs a camera and a microphone)
 - 1 participant
 - Original robot's program
 - Modified robot's program
 - Godspeed Questionnaire
 - Negative Attitudes Toward Robots Questionnaire
 - Note taking tools

Randomly set the order of testing the original and modified versions. (First modified, then original or other way around.)


The participant and the experimenter are alone in a closed room (as little noise as possible is great).

Legal and ethical agreements made here.

(If possible) Start the computers microphone and camera to make a video recording with sound using the same tools as the robot will.



Inform the participant that they will be talking to a "robot" which is a program that uses that microphone to record sound, translate it to text, send to an local LLM which generates a response, which is then "read aloud" by the robot. Inform the participant that the robot also uses the camera to see the participant during the conversation and uses that information in its algorithm.
Inform the participant, that the robot only speaks english.

Inform the participant that before the conversations begin, they will be interviewed regarding their attitude towards robots.

Inform the participant that they will be testing 2 different version of the robot, with a short questionaire after each conversation.

Inform the participant that each conversation will take about 3-4 minutes, with a "godspeed" questionaie taking about 5 minutes each. 

Ask the participant to categorize their ability in english from: non-able, fluent/native, or somewhere between.

Have the participant fill out the Negative Attitudes Toward Robots Questionnaire, by checking off statements they agree with.

During each conversation, the experimenter will not speak. They will turn off the robot after 3-4 minutes.

During the godspeed questionaire, if the participant does not understand one of the points, they should leave it blank.

After both conversationgs, ask the participant which they prefered, if any, and why.

After the experiment, thank the participant, and explain that the 2 version were an original and an attempted improved version. Ask the participant if they are able to identify which is which.

Thank the participant, and make sure that the participant gets a copy of the Legal and ethical agreements.
